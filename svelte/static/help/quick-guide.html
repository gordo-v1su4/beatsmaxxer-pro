START HERE / BEFORE PLAYBACK

Make a Timing first pass.

Load a song and clips. Let musical events shape their motion. Record the cuts, review the take, then export a video or an editable Resolve timeline.

First: let the song and clips finish loading.

Look for green RHY·OK and ARR·OK in the top bar. Blue/loading is still working. Read preparation or missing-media messages before recording.

  1. Try the complete project

    Top bar → WORKFLOW → REDLINE · COMPLETE TIMING DEMO. It loads a song, real clips, musical data, sections, clip banks and an example take.

  2. Find your workspace

    TIMING shapes playback speed and source time. ARRANGE shows cuts and sections. PERFORM is a separate effects workspace; see Where Perform fits.

  3. Watch the program

    Press top-bar PLAY. The large program monitor is the output; ON AIR identifies its deck. Moving previews do not mean all decks are on air.

  4. Review the demo take

    Open ARRANGE, enable its PLAY mode, then start top-bar PLAY. Arrangement PLAY recalls recorded choices and settings.

Using your own files

Load audio with SONG and videos with CLIPS. Wait for analysis and preparation. MIDI NOTES needs imported note data; RHY·OK does not create MIDI notes. If a channel is missing, import matching data or choose an available trigger source.

Keep originals. Workflow and arrangement files store project data and references, not embedded copies of all your media.

TOP BAR → TIMING / EDIT MOTION

Change when video frames play.

Timing changes speed, repeats and source position. It adds no LUM, RGB, WARP or opacity mix. Default Triggered Sample returns to normal advancing playback between accepted effects.

  1. Choose a deck

    Click its timing header. FOLLOW ON AIR makes the editor follow the program; leave it off to keep one deck selected.

  2. Choose scope

    GLOBAL shares Timing settings; MODULAR edits videos separately. Each video keeps its assigned effect.

  3. Choose SPEEDRAMP, STUTTER or TIMESAMPLER

    These are the three Timing tabs. OFF bypasses the selected effect.

  4. Choose what starts it

    Set SOURCE and CHANNEL in shared Timing triggers. Try MIDI NOTES → VOCALS in the demo. A note offers an opportunity, not a guaranteed activation.

Speedramp

The curve is speed over a cycle: 1× normal, 0.25× quarter speed, 2× double speed. CYCLE sets duration. SNAP aligns curve points during editing, not trigger times. Fixed uses one cycle length; Random chooses from selected lengths. Ramp alone uses verified 96 FPS preparation for smoother slow motion.

Stutter

Repeat a short piece, hold a frame, or jump between pieces. Division sets the slice length; repeats make a burst. By Energy varies repeats with event strength. Stutter uses original source frames.

Timesampler: what should happen

Default TRIGGERED SAMPLE plays normally until an event is accepted. At its launch boundary it captures one source slice, plays that sample’s motion for the requested loops, then rejoins the normal playhead—which has kept advancing.

SLICES4 / 8 / 16 / 32 source regions. More slices means a shorter region for the same clip.
LOOPHow many times the captured motion plays before returning to normal.
RATESpeed inside a sample, 0.25×–2×. Slower lasts longer. Normal playback between samples stays at 1×.
FWD / REV / PONGTriggered Sample plays the region forward, backward, or forward then backward.
RND / 3 STEP / 5 STEPSeeded random or structured source-anchor choices. These are slice positions, not musical pitch intervals.
LAUNCHAccepted events wait for NEXT BEAT or NEXT BAR.
FINISH LOOPIgnore incoming notes until all active sample loops finish. Default.
RESTART ON TRIGGERAnother accepted note may interrupt and restart at the selected launch boundary.
Other behaviorsTRIGGERED SEQUENCE traverses a phrase of slices; REV/PONG change slice order there. ORIGINAL · CONTINUOUS retains the continuously cycling behavior.
Reading the sampler picture

Gray = original source positions. Cyan = sampled output. The diagonal marks the current jump into the sample, not a fade or shader. NORMAL / QUEUED / SAMPLING tells you when it is active.

Try: MIDI NOTES → VOCALS, Triggered Sample, REV, 8 slices, 1 loop, 1×. CHANCE 0% should play normally. At 100%, eligible notes launch backward samples. Compare both retrigger modes. A jump before an accepted trigger, or an unexpected interruption in Finish Loop, is behavior to investigate.

TIMING / READ MUSICAL LENGTHS

Beats, bars, and the whole curve.

In the app’s 4/4 grid, four beats make a bar. At 120 BPM, one beat lasts 0.5 seconds and a bar lasts 2 seconds. Tempo changes seconds, not beat counts.

Beat-grid EVERY offers trigger opportunities. Ramp CYCLE sets motion length. Sampler LAUNCH aligns its start. SNAP aligns edited points. None is a dedicated minimum/maximum shot-length control.

TOP BAR → ARRANGE / REVIEW THE TIMING FIRST PASS

Read cuts, sections and banks.

Each lane is a deck. A filled block means that deck is on air for that span. Width is shot duration; timing motion happens inside the shot.

Why S0, S1, S2…?

CUT ORDER → BALANCED favors the eligible deck shown least recently. Similar schedules can produce a cycle. RANDOM chooses among eligible decks using SEED, avoiding the current deck when another is eligible. One eligible deck still gives only one choice.

A saved take keeps its recorded order.

CUT ORDER does not shuffle existing cuts. Save your current arrangement, exit arrangement PLAY, choose Random in live Timing and record a new pass to capture it.

Why are the shot lengths so even?

The scheduler waits for the chosen effect’s busy period before accepting another automatic program cut. Shared cycle lengths, musical boundaries and regular events can create regular cuts. A deck stays on air until a later accepted cut, so a shot can outlast its effect. Random changes the deck, not shot duration independently.

Density, gaps and motion lengths influence spacing. There is no dedicated minimum/maximum shot-length editor here. More varied pacing with protection against tiny cuts remains a separate design question.

Sections recall clip banks

A named bank is a reusable clip set assigned to rack slots. A section can recall that bank at its boundary. Verse and Chorus are labels, not restrictions: any suitable section may use the same bank.

  1. Prepare banks while stopped

    In CLIPS create/fill a set or capture the rack. Assign it with each section’s clip-set selector above the lanes. Use clear names such as Verse · Bank A.

  2. Record

    Choose REC in ARRANGE, then top-bar PLAY. Cuts write to lanes. A new pass replaces cuts under the playhead unless OVERDUB is on.

  3. Review

    Stop, choose arrangement PLAY, then top-bar PLAY. It replays recorded cuts and snapshots. Click the active mode again to return to live editing.

  4. Save

    DOWNLOAD ARRANGEMENT saves JSON project data. LOAD ARRANGEMENT restores it. Neither renders video nor exports a Resolve timeline.

Clip-set edits are locked during REC/PLAY. FIT ALL shows the song. +/− or the timeline wheel zooms. LOOP uses the selected section range. Save before replacing clips or recording again.

TOP BAR → ARRANGE → EXPORT

Download a finished video.

EXPORT is in the Arrangement toolbar above the monitor and lanes. It opens the slim EXPORT VIDEO row. DOWNLOAD ARRANGEMENT is a separate project save.

  1. Check the take

    Review with arrangement PLAY. Resolve missing clips and finish preparation.

  2. Choose SONG or LOOP

    SONG is the whole track. LOOP is the defined loop; if disabled, set a loop first.

  3. Choose FPS

    24 matches the demo’s 24 FPS originals. Higher FPS does not invent source detail.

  4. Choose output

    RESOLVE TIMELINE off = finished video. On = the Resolve ZIP, including an editable timeline, reference video and audio.

  5. START EXPORT, then wait

    Preparing → real-time recording → finalizing at selected FPS. Finalizing takes extra time after the song ends. Keep the app open. CANCEL stops the job.

  6. Save the result

    Download begins when ready. DOWNLOAD LAST VIDEO or DOWNLOAD RESOLVE ZIP remains available. Your browser chooses the folder or Save dialog.

Small preview, original-resolution Timing export.

Timing reads original/prepared source frames rather than enlarging its tiny preview. Keep originals available. The demo sources are 1280×720; higher FPS does not make them 1080p.

TIMING → ARRANGE → EXPORT → RESOLVE TIMELINE

Continue the Timing edit in Resolve.

Transfer cuts, source-frame choices, reverse, holds, speed changes and song audio. This does not transfer editable Perform shaders.

  1. Export the ZIP

    In ARRANGE open EXPORT. Choose SONG/LOOP and FPS, enable RESOLVE TIMELINE, then START EXPORT. When ready, DOWNLOAD RESOLVE ZIP.

  2. Extract it

    Keep the folder together: .fcpxml timeline, reference video, processed song WAV, source manifest, README and Media folder.

  3. Keep originals nearby

    The ZIP references original clips by filename; it does not duplicate all originals. Keep matching names. Retain included prepared interpolation clips too.

  4. Import in Resolve

    Open a project → File → Import → Timeline → select the extracted .fcpxml. Use the matching timeline frame rate.

  5. Relink

    Point Resolve to originals and the extracted Media folder. If clips are offline, select them in the Media Pool and use Relink Selected Clips. Avoid renaming files during relinking.

  6. Compare, then edit

    Compare against the reference: cuts, reverse, holds, ending and audio sync. Then trim, grade and finish in Resolve.

Test a short range first.

Resolve 21.1 was tested with the real 40.9-second, 982-frame two-mode demo take at 720p/24 and aligned audio. Full-song native video export passed separately. Other formats, rates and Resolve versions still need checking.

Why many small pieces in Resolve?

The timeline describes discontinuities, holds and reversals with retimed pieces or nested source ranges. A small piece is not necessarily an accidental flash cut. Compare the rendered picture against the reference before simplifying it.

Which effects are not editable there?

Perform shaders, color filters and opacity effects are not encoded in this Timing timeline. FCPXML is an interchange file, not a universal plugin format.

TOP BAR → PERFORM / SEPARATE EFFECTS WORKSPACE

Where Perform fits.

Perform is the original playground: one visual effect module per deck, musical controls and live cuts. Timing focuses on the motion and pacing of a first pass.

WorkspacePurposeArrange
TIMINGSpeedramp, Stutter, Timesampler. Clean picture, changed source time.Records/replays Timing cuts and snapshots.
PERFORMVisual modules, parameters and mix.Records/replays the selected Perform workspace.
ARRANGESections, clip banks, recorded take.Keeps the selected rendering workspace; not a third effect engine.
Switching tabs is not a finished Timing → Perform pipeline.

Stop before switching. A Timing take does not automatically become a combined stack of Timing and editable Perform effects. Save your first pass. The finishing handoff remains a separate product review.

Perform currently exports the rendered monitor with its effects. Timing has original-resolution source-frame export and Resolve interchange. Do not assume both paths have identical export resolution or interchange support.
