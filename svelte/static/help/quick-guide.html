What makes an effect happen?

A trigger is a musical event that can start an effect. An onset is the start of a sound, such as a drum hit or a vocal syllable. Mix means the whole song.

Source / Channel
MIDI follows note starts. Audio sources follow the whole song or a selected stem. Vocals follows that part; Channels by Deck distributes available channels across decks.
Threshold
Minimum trigger strength, from 0 to 1. Raise it to ignore weaker events.
Density
Keeps the strongest share in each channel. 40% keeps the strongest 40%. Lower density also adds spacing after an effect: (100% − Density) × 2 beats.
Ramp Chance
The share of eligible events that start a Speedramp. 40% does not shorten the curve.
Gap
Normal-playback time after the effect, plus Density’s extra spacing. 180 ms is 0.18 seconds. Events during the effect or gap are ignored.
Which source should I choose?

MIDI: written note starts. Mix onsets: attacks anywhere in the song. Vocal or stem onsets: attacks in that isolated part. Vocal phrase starts: entrances after quiet gaps. RMS peaks: broader loud peaks. Beat: regular musical intervals. PGM: the deck becoming the on-air picture. Continuous: cycles without waiting for a trigger.

Global, Modular and Follow on Air

Global shares parameters across decks while keeping their Speedramp or Stutter assignment. Modular edits one deck separately. Follow on Air makes the editor follow the deck on PGM; turn it off to keep editing one deck.

With MIDI or audio triggers, accepted bursts drive automatic picture changes. Manual cuts take priority. The upper random/interval/feel controls are inactive for those musical sources.

Beats, bars and effect length

In the app’s 4/4 grid, four beats make one bar. Two beats is half a bar. At 120 BPM, one beat lasts half a second.

Speedramp: Cycle sets the duration

Two bars means one curve lasting eight beats. Four bars means sixteen beats. Fixed uses one length; Random chooses from your selected lengths for each accepted trigger.

Stutter: Division and repeats make a burst

A 1/8 slice is half a beat. Repeating it three times makes a burst of one and a half beats. By Energy chooses repeat patterns from the trigger’s strength.

Cycle, Every, Snap and speed range

Cycle sets the ramp’s duration. Every, under Beat, sets how often beat events are offered; an active effect still has to finish. Snap aligns the points while you edit the curve. It does not change when the effect is triggered.

0.25× is quarter speed, 1× is normal and 2× is double speed. Min × and Max × rescale the actual curve. Continuous cycles without note or onset triggers; density, chance and gaps do not apply.

Record it. Check it. Export it.

Arrange follows the song’s sections and keeps the cuts and snapshots for your take.

Build your own banks
In the CLIPS browser, add a clip set, fill its slots or capture the current rack, then assign that same set to the relevant song sections. One verse bank can serve every verse.
REC
Writes on-air cuts to the timeline and captures section clips and take snapshots. A new pass replaces cuts under the playhead unless Overdub is on.
PLAY
The recorded cuts and snapshots drive the picture. The app still renders playback; the exported video is the finished file. Clip-set edits are locked during REC and PLAY. Click the active mode again to return to your workspace.
Save / Load
Download Arrangement saves the take, clip sets and assignments. It does not embed song or video files; keep the originals available.
Export
Choose the whole song or selected loop and an output frame rate, normally 24 FPS. Export uses the current monitor size. Once the video is ready, the download starts automatically; browser settings choose its folder or show a Save dialog.
